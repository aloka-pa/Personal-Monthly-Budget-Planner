Understanding your score

The Financial Health Score on your Dashboard is a single number from 0–100 that summarizes four separate habits: how well you stuck to your budgets, how much you saved, how evenly you spread out your spending, and how you're keeping up with your savings goals.

This page exists so you never have to take the score on faith. Every formula, benchmark, and rule below is exactly what Wallet Check uses to produce the number you see — nothing is simplified in a way that changes what it means. If a section feels technical, the plain-language explanation above each formula is enough on its own to understand what's going on.

How is the score calculated?

Which month does it cover?

Your score always covers the most recently fully completed calendar month — never the month currently in progress, since it hasn't finished yet and would be an unfair, incomplete picture. For example, if today is any day in August, your score covers July 1–31, and it's compared against June.

Four independent components

Budgeting, Saving, Spending Consistency, and Goals are each calculated completely separately, from different parts of your data, using the rules below. They're then combined into one Overall Score using the fixed weights shown in Score Weights.

When data is missing

If a component can't be calculated for the month — for example you have no goals, or no budgets configured — it's shown as "—" instead of being scored. It is never silently counted as a 0. The Overall Score is then calculated using only the components you do have, reweighted so they still add up fairly. The full rule is in Overall Score.

Score weights

Each component contributes this share of your Overall Score, when all four are available:

Budgeting 30%
Saving 30%
Spending Consistency 20%
Goals 20%

Budgeting and Saving carry more weight because they reflect your core month-to-month cash flow. Spending Consistency and Goals carry less. These weights are fixed by Wallet Check — you can't change them.

1. Budgeting Score — weight 30%

What it measures

How closely your spending matched the monthly budgets you set for each category, and whether your spending actually happened in categories that have a budget in the first place.

Only categories with "Include in Budget" switched on are counted, and only entries you logged as an Expense — anything logged as a Saving entry (money you deliberately set aside, like a transfer into a savings account) is never treated as spending, here or anywhere else in the score.

How it is calculated

Budgeting combines two measurements:

a) Category Adherence — for each budgeted category, compares what you spent to what you budgeted:

Spent at or under budget → Adherence = 100 Spent over budget → Adherence = 100 × (Budget ÷ Amount Spent)

The further over budget you go, the lower that category's Adherence falls. Every category's Adherence is then combined into one number, weighted by how much you actually spent in each category — so a category you barely touch doesn't count as much as the category holding most of your spending.

Weighted Adherence = Σ (Category Adherence × Category Spending) ───────────────────────────────────────────── Σ (Category Spending)
Example Food: budget 1,000, spent 1,200 → Adherence 83.3
Bills: budget 3,000, spent 3,000 → Adherence 100
Weighted Adherence = (83.3 × 1,200 + 100 × 3,000) ÷ 4,200 = 95.2

If you had budgets configured but spent nothing at all in those categories, this part scores 100 — there was nothing to overspend.

b) Budget Coverage — checks whether your spending stayed inside categories that have a configured budget, instead of leaking into budget-tracked categories that don't have one set up yet:

Budget Coverage = Spending in categories that have a configured budget ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────── × 100 All spending in "Include in Budget" categories

If there was no spending at all in "Include in Budget" categories that month, Coverage = 100.

These two combine into the final Budgeting Score:

Budgeting Score = (Weighted Adherence × 0.80) + (Budget Coverage × 0.20)

What affects it

  • Overspending any budgeted category lowers Adherence — the category with the biggest overspend (weighted by amount) hurts the most.
  • Spending in an "Include in Budget" category that has no configured budget lowers Coverage, even if you didn't technically overspend anywhere.
  • A category budgeted at 0 that still has spending pulls that category's Adherence all the way to 0.
Availability: Budgeting is only shown when at least one "Include in Budget" category has a positive budget configured for that month. Otherwise it shows "—", not 0.

2. Saving Score — weight 30%

What it measures

The share of your income you kept — i.e. didn't spend — that month.

Benchmark

Wallet Check treats a 20% savings rate as the target for a perfect score. This benchmark is fixed by the app; it isn't something you configure.

How it is calculated

Step 1 — Savings Rate:

Savings Rate = (Income − Expense-type Spending) ÷ Income

"Expense-type Spending" only includes entries logged as Expense that month. Entries logged as Saving (money you deliberately set aside — e.g. a transfer to a savings account, or an insurance payment) are not subtracted here. That money is treated as retained, not spent, so it naturally raises your effective savings rate.

Step 2 — Saving Score:

Saving Score = (Savings Rate ÷ 20%) × 100, kept within 0–100
Savings rateSaving Score
0% or less0
5%25
10%50
15%75
20% or more100
Availability: Saving is only shown when you've entered a positive income amount for that month. Otherwise it shows "—" — a missing income entry is never scored as a 0.

3. Spending Consistency Score — weight 20%

What it measures

Whether your day-to-day spending stayed level and predictable across the month, rather than a few big spending days pushing you over — even if your total for the month looked fine.

How it is calculated

First, Wallet Check works out how much you'd need to spend per day to use your budgets evenly:

Daily Target = Sum of your "Include in Budget" category budgets ─────────────────────────────────────────────────── Number of days in the month

Then, for every day in the month:

Daily Spend = that day's Expense-type spending in "Include in Budget" categories A day passes when: Daily Spend ≤ Daily Target (a day with no spending always passes)
Spending Consistency Score = (Days that passed ÷ Days in month) × 100
Example Monthly budget 60,000 over 30 days → Daily Target 2,000
27 out of 30 days stayed at or under 2,000
Score = 27 ÷ 30 × 100 = 90

What lowers it

Any day where your budget-tracked Expense spending went over the daily target — including a single large one-off purchase. A big purchase only fails the one day it happened on; it doesn't reduce the days that follow.

Availability: needs the same thing Budgeting does — at least one positive budget on an "Include in Budget" category that month. Otherwise "—".

4. Goals Score — weight 20%

What it measures

How much you contributed toward your active savings goals that month, compared to how much those goals combined needed that month to stay on schedule. "Active" means any goal you haven't marked completed.

How expected monthly contributions are determined

Every active goal gets an "expected monthly pace" — how much it should ideally receive each month:

Goal has a target date: Expected Pace = Target Amount ÷ months between goal creation and target date Goal has no target date (or an unusable one): Expected Pace = Target Amount ÷ 12 (a flat, assumed one-year pace)

The 12-month fallback exists so a goal without a deadline still has a reasonable, non-zero pace instead of being excluded — the same idea as Saving's flat 20% benchmark.

All active goals' expected paces are added together into one combined total:

Expected Monthly Pace Total = Σ (Expected Pace) across all active goals

How it is calculated

Every contribution dated within the score month is added up across all your active goals — it doesn't matter which specific goal received it, or when that goal was created:

Contributed Total = Σ (contributions dated in the score month) across active goals Goals Score = (Contributed Total ÷ Expected Monthly Pace Total) × 100, kept within 0–100
Example Goal A: target 50,000, 10 months to go → pace 5,000/mo
Goal B: target 20,000, 8 months to go → pace 2,500/mo
Goal C: target 5,000, 3 months to go → pace 1,667/mo
Combined expected pace = 9,167
You contributed 15,000 this month, all to Goal A
Goals Score = 15,000 ÷ 9,167 × 100 = 100 (capped)
Because contributions and expected pace are added up together across all your goals (not scored per goal and averaged), one large contribution to a single goal can fully cover the month's expected pace even if your other goals received nothing that month. This is intentional — it rewards the total amount you put toward your goals, not how evenly you spread it across them.
A note on completed goals: whether a goal counts as "active" is based on its current completed status, even when looking back at a past month. Marking a goal completed today removes it from every month's Goals calculation, including months you've already seen scored — so an old month's Goals score can shift slightly if you recalculate it after completing a goal. This is a deliberate trade-off: Wallet Check doesn't keep a history of exactly when a goal was completed, so it uses the best information available rather than guessing.
Availability: needs at least one active goal with a usable, positive target amount. Otherwise "—".

Overall Score

When all four components are available, they're combined using the weights from Score Weights:

Overall Score = (Budgeting × 30%) + (Saving × 30%) + (Spending Consistency × 20%) + (Goals × 20%)

When a component is missing

A component that shows "—" is removed entirely from the calculation — it is never treated as a 0. The remaining components' weights are scaled up so they still add to a fair total:

Overall Score = Σ (Available Component Score × its Weight) ───────────────────────────────────────────── Σ (Weights of the available components only)
Example — Spending Consistency unavailable that month Budgeting: 80 (weight 30) · Saving: 70 (weight 30) · Goals: 90 (weight 20)
Overall = (80×30 + 70×30 + 90×20) ÷ (30+30+20) = 6,300 ÷ 80 = 78.75 → 79

Minimum data needed to show an Overall Score

An Overall Score only appears when both of these are true:

  1. At least one of Budgeting or Saving is available (Wallet Check treats these as the two "core" habits), and
  2. At least one more component (of any of the four) is also available.

If the minimum isn't met, no Overall Score is shown — just "—" — but whichever individual components you do have are still displayed. See Why is my score unavailable?

The "vs last month" indicator

The small up/down arrow next to your score compares this month's Overall Score to last month's. It only appears when both months have a valid Overall Score and the exact same set of components was available in both. If your data coverage changed between the two months (for example, you added a budget this month that you didn't have configured last month), the comparison is hidden and replaced with "Comparison unavailable — data coverage changed" — rather than showing a number that isn't really comparing like with like.

What affects my score?

Under "What affected your score" on your Dashboard, Wallet Check picks from exactly these statements, only showing the ones that actually apply to your month:

  • "Savings rate was below the 20% benchmark." — Saving is available and your rate was under 20%.
  • "One or more categories exceeded their configured budgets." — Budgeting is available and your Weighted Adherence was under 100.
  • "Some budget-enabled spending occurred in categories without budgets." — Budgeting is available and your Budget Coverage was under 100.
  • "A number of days exceeded the daily spending target." — Spending Consistency is available and at least one day went over target.
  • "Contributions to active goals fell short of their combined expected monthly pace." — Goals is available and your Goals Score was under 100.

If none of these apply, you'll see "Your finances were on track this month." instead.

Why did my score change?

When a valid month-to-month comparison exists (see Overall Score) and your rounded score actually moved, the Dashboard switches to cause-and-effect wording:

  • "Your score improved because…" followed by the specific components that scored higher than last month.
  • "Your score decreased because…" followed by the specific components that scored lower than last month.

Only components that actually moved in that direction are listed — for example, if your score dropped but your Saving component improved, Saving will not be blamed for the drop. When there's no valid comparison (or the score didn't move), you'll see the neutral "What affected your score" list instead, describing your current month on its own terms rather than implying a trend that can't be proven.

Why is my score unavailable?

Each component needs a minimum amount of data before it can be calculated at all:

ComponentAvailable when…
BudgetingAt least one "Include in Budget" category has a positive budget that month.
SavingYou've entered a positive income amount for that month.
Spending ConsistencySame requirement as Budgeting — a positive budget on an "Include in Budget" category.
GoalsYou have at least one active (not completed) goal with a usable, positive target amount.

A missing component always shows "—", never a 0 — a 0 would unfairly suggest bad financial behavior when really the data just isn't there yet.

The Overall Score itself needs at least one of Budgeting/Saving available, plus one more component of any kind — otherwise it's also shown as "—", even though your individual available components still display normally.
